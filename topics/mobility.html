5G Mobility & Handover

A practical way to think about measurements, decisions and handover failures.

Why mobility is difficult

Mobility is an interaction between radio measurements, UE behaviour, network configuration and signalling. A handover failure can therefore have a radio trigger but a protocol-level symptom.

Useful investigation sequence

  1. Check serving and neighbour measurements.
  2. Identify the event that triggered the mobility decision.
  3. Follow the handover preparation signalling.
  4. Check target-cell access and reconfiguration.
  5. Check whether user-plane continuity was maintained.
Practical rule: always capture both the serving-cell and target-cell context when investigating a handover issue.

What a handover is trying to achieve

A handover moves an active connection from one cell to another while trying to keep the service usable. The network uses measurements and configuration to decide when mobility should happen, then coordinates preparation and execution.

Why failures are tricky

A handover can fail because of radio conditions, configuration, signalling, timing or target-cell access. The visible symptom may appear at a higher layer than the original cause, so the investigation should follow the complete procedure.

Practical sequence

Compare serving and neighbour measurements, identify the mobility trigger, follow preparation signalling, inspect target-cell access and reconfiguration, and finally verify user-plane continuity. This gives you a consistent troubleshooting workflow.
