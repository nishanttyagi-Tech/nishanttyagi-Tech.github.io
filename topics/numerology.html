5G NR Numerology

Understand subcarrier spacing, symbols, slots and why NR timing is flexible.

What is numerology?

In NR, numerology is mainly associated with the selected subcarrier spacing. 3GPP defines the numerology using a scaling relationship from a reference spacing. Changing it changes the time structure of the radio grid.

Common SCSSimple idea
15 kHzLonger symbols and a larger slot duration.
30 kHzShorter symbols and shorter slots than 15 kHz.
60 kHzUseful where a tighter time structure is needed.
120 kHzUsed in higher-frequency / specialized NR deployments.

For the standard relationship, the subcarrier spacing scales as 15 × 2μ kHz, where μ is the numerology index.

Why engineers care

  • It changes the time-frequency structure.
  • It affects slot timing and scheduling.
  • It appears in configuration and measurements.
  • It matters when correlating RF measurements with protocol events.
Reference: 3GPP TS 38.211 covers physical channels, modulation and the NR resource grid.

The basic idea

NR does not use one fixed time-frequency structure for every deployment. Subcarrier spacing can be selected according to the radio scenario. A larger spacing gives shorter OFDM symbols and a shorter basic time structure. This flexibility helps NR support different frequency ranges and latency requirements.

Why 15, 30, 60 and 120 kHz matter

For a beginner, remember the direction rather than memorizing numbers: when SCS increases, symbols become shorter and the slot structure becomes faster. The commonly encountered values are 15, 30, 60 and 120 kHz, with higher values used in suitable deployments.

Reading a log

When you see numerology-related information in a UE log, look for SCS, bandwidth, BWP and timing together. These values describe the radio configuration as a system; looking at only one value can lead to a wrong interpretation.
