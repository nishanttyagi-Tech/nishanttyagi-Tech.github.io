5G / NEW RADIO

5G / NR Complete Engineering Guide

A structured technical reference covering the radio interface, NG-RAN, 5G Core, signalling, procedures, QoS, slicing, mobility, voice, planning, troubleshooting and 5G-Advanced evolution.

01

5G / NR Fundamentals

5G combines a flexible radio interface (NR) with a service-based 5G Core. The design targets enhanced mobile broadband, ultra-reliable low-latency services, massive machine communication, industrial connectivity and flexible deployment.

02

5G Architecture

UE→NG-RAN→5GC→Data / Service

The end-to-end model is UE → NR/NG-RAN → 5GC → Data Network. SA uses NR with 5GC; NSA uses NR together with LTE/EPC or other migration architectures. The RAN provides radio access while the core provides registration, authentication, mobility, session management, policy and user-plane connectivity.

03

UE

The UE contains RF, modem/PHY, MAC, RLC, PDCP, SDAP and RRC functions plus NAS procedures toward the core. Capability signalling tells the network which bands, bandwidths, MIMO layers, features and voice capabilities are supported.

04

NG-RAN

UE→NG-RAN→5GC→Data / Service

NG-RAN is built from gNBs and, where applicable, ng-eNBs. gNBs connect to the 5GC over NG and to neighbouring RAN nodes over Xn. A gNB may be centralized or split into CU and DU.

05

gNB Architecture

A split gNB commonly contains CU-CP, CU-UP and DU. F1 connects CU and DU; E1 connects CU-CP and CU-UP. This separation allows centralized control/user-plane functions and distributed real-time radio processing.

06

5G Core Network

UE→NG-RAN→5GC→Data / Service

The principal functions are AMF, SMF, UPF, AUSF, UDM, PCF, NRF, NSSF and NEF. The architecture uses service-based interfaces between control-plane network functions and separate user-plane forwarding.

07

AMF / SMF / UPF

AMF handles access and mobility; SMF controls PDU sessions and UPF selection; UPF forwards user traffic and anchors traffic toward external data networks. Keeping control and forwarding separate enables distributed UPF deployment.

08

Other 5GC Functions

AUSF supports authentication, UDM stores subscription-related data, PCF provides policy, NRF provides NF discovery, NSSF assists slice selection, and NEF exposes controlled network capabilities.

09

Interfaces

UE→NG-RAN→5GC→Data / Service

Important interfaces include Uu, NG/N2/N3, Xn, F1, E1, N1, N2, N3, N4 and N6. NG-C uses NGAP over SCTP/IP; NG-U uses GTP-U over UDP/IP. Xn supports RAN coordination and mobility.

10

Protocol Stack

UE→NG-RAN→5GC→Data / Service

The radio stack includes RRC, PDCP, RLC, MAC and PHY; SDAP maps QoS flows to data radio bearers. NAS operates between UE and core functions. NGAP carries control signalling between NG-RAN and AMF.

11

Standalone and Non-Standalone

SA uses NR plus 5GC. NSA allows NR to be introduced while LTE/EPC or migration architectures remain part of the service path. EN-DC uses LTE as master and NR as secondary; MCG, SCG and split bearers are important concepts.

12

Multi-Connectivity

UE→NG-RAN→5GC→Data / Service

Multi-connectivity can combine master and secondary nodes. Bearer choices include MCG bearer, SCG bearer and split bearer, allowing traffic to use LTE, NR or both depending on configuration.

13

Spectrum and Frequency Ranges

NR supports Frequency Range 1 and Frequency Range 2. Deployment choices affect propagation, coverage, bandwidth, beam management, antenna design and achievable throughput.

14

NR-ARFCN and GSCN

NR-ARFCN identifies carrier frequency using the NR raster. GSCN identifies synchronization raster positions used when locating SS/PBCH blocks.

15

Numerology

NR scales OFDM subcarrier spacing using μ, with Δf = 15 kHz × 2^μ. Higher numerology shortens symbol and slot duration and is useful where shorter transmission intervals are required.

16

Frame and Slot Structure

A radio frame is 10 ms. Slot duration depends on numerology. A normal-CP slot contains 14 OFDM symbols. Flexible slot formats support DL, UL and special/flexible symbol allocation.

17

Resource Grid

The basic time-frequency unit is the Resource Element. Twelve consecutive subcarriers form a Resource Block. Bandwidth Parts allow a UE to operate on a configured subset of the carrier resource grid.

18

Bandwidth Parts

BWP can reduce UE active bandwidth and support different operational configurations. A UE may have configured DL and UL BWPs and can switch between them using control signalling.

19

Synchronization

The UE detects SS/PBCH blocks to obtain synchronization and identify the serving cell. PSS and SSS support synchronization/cell identity acquisition; PBCH carries essential broadcast information.

20

SSB / PSS / SSS / PBCH

An SS/PBCH block contains PSS, SSS and PBCH. Multiple SSBs can represent different beams. The UE uses SSB measurements for synchronization and beam/cell selection.

21

System Information

MIB is carried through PBCH and provides essential physical-layer/system information. SIBs provide additional configuration such as cell access, serving-cell configuration and common radio parameters.

22

Downlink Channels

PDCCH carries downlink control information, while PDSCH carries downlink shared data. Scheduling decisions determine time-frequency resources, modulation/coding and HARQ processes.

23

Uplink Channels

PRACH provides random access, PUCCH carries uplink control information, PUSCH carries uplink shared data, and SRS supports uplink channel sounding and related measurements.

24

Reference Signals

NR uses DM-RS, CSI-RS, PT-RS and SRS for demodulation, channel measurement, phase tracking and uplink channel sounding. Their placement and purpose differ by channel and procedure.

25

MIMO

MIMO uses multiple antenna ports/layers to improve throughput, robustness or coverage. Rank, precoding, codebooks and channel-state information influence the selected transmission.

26

Beamforming and Beam Management

Massive-MIMO systems form directional beams. Beam sweeping, measurement, reporting, selection, refinement and recovery maintain a suitable serving beam, especially at higher frequencies.

27

CSI

Channel State Information includes measurements and feedback used for scheduling and MIMO. CSI reporting can include CQI, PMI and RI, depending on configuration.

28

Modulation and Coding

NR supports QPSK, 16QAM, 64QAM and higher-order modulation depending on configuration and capability. LDPC is used for shared-channel data coding; Polar coding is used for relevant control/broadcast information.

29

MAC

MAC performs multiplexing, logical-channel prioritization, scheduling-related control, HARQ operation, random access control and MAC control elements such as BSR and PHR.

30

RLC

RLC provides transparent, unacknowledged and acknowledged modes. Functions include segmentation/reassembly, retransmission in AM, sequence handling and reordering as applicable.

31

PDCP

PDCP provides sequence handling, reordering, duplication-related functions, header compression where configured, ciphering and integrity protection for applicable traffic.

32

SDAP

SDAP maps 5G QoS flows to data radio bearers and can mark QoS-flow identity in the user plane. This creates the hierarchy PDU Session → QoS Flow → DRB.

33

SR / BSR / PHR

Scheduling Request tells the gNB that uplink resources are needed. Buffer Status Report describes queued uplink data. Power Headroom Report indicates remaining transmit-power margin.

34

HARQ

Hybrid ARQ combines forward-error correction with retransmission. NR supports multiple parallel HARQ processes so new transmissions can continue while earlier data awaits acknowledgement.

35

Random Access / PRACH

Random access establishes uplink timing and initial access. The contention-based procedure is commonly described as Msg1 PRACH preamble, Msg2 Random Access Response, Msg3 transmission and contention resolution.

36

RRC States

NR uses RRC_IDLE, RRC_INACTIVE and RRC_CONNECTED. Inactive enables efficient suspension/resumption while retaining useful UE context and reducing signalling compared with a complete reconnect.

37

RRC Signalling Radio Bearers

SRB0 supports initial RRC common signalling, SRB1 supports dedicated RRC signalling and initial NAS transfer, SRB2 carries dedicated NAS signalling after security activation, and SRB3 can support specific secondary-node signalling scenarios.

38

RRC Connection Establishment

A simplified flow is RRCSetupRequest → RRCSetup → RRCSetupComplete. NAS registration information may be carried through the RRC signalling path toward the AMF.

39

Registration

5G registration includes registration request, identity handling where needed, authentication, NAS security, registration acceptance and completion. The UE may identify itself with a concealed identity such as SUCI.

40

Authentication and Security

5G security includes primary authentication, NAS security, access-stratum security, integrity protection and ciphering. SUPI is the permanent subscription identity, SUCI conceals it over the radio, and 5G-GUTI provides a temporary identity.

41

PDU Session

UE→NG-RAN→5GC→Data / Service

A PDU Session provides connectivity between UE and a DNN through the 5GC. It can carry multiple QoS flows and can support IPv4, IPv6, IPv4v6, Ethernet or unstructured traffic depending on configuration.

42

PDU Session Establishment

UE→NG-RAN→5GC→Data / Service

The UE requests a PDU Session; AMF transfers session-management signalling toward SMF; SMF selects/configures UPF and policy; the RAN receives resource configuration; the UE receives the PDU Session Establishment Accept.

43

QoS and 5QI

UE→NG-RAN→5GC→Data / Service

A QoS Flow is the finest QoS granularity in the 5G user plane. 5QI identifies standardized or operator-specific QoS characteristics. QoS flows are mapped to DRBs by SDAP and ultimately to transport tunnels.

44

Network Slicing

UE→NG-RAN→5GC→Data / Service

A network slice is a logical network tailored to a service requirement. S-NSSAI identifies slice information using SST and optional SD. Slice selection involves UE subscription, AMF/NSSF logic and the availability of suitable network functions.

45

Mobility and Cell Reselection

Mobility includes intra-frequency, inter-frequency and inter-RAT cases. Connected-mode mobility relies on measurements and RRC procedures; idle/inactive mobility uses cell reselection rules.

46

Handover

UE→NG-RAN→5GC→Data / Service

Xn-based and NG-based handovers involve preparation, target-resource admission, UE execution, synchronization, path switching and data forwarding. Conditional handover and beam mobility extend the mobility toolbox.

47

VoNR

UE→NG-RAN→5GC→Data / Service

VoNR uses NR access with 5GC and IMS. SIP provides call signalling, SDP negotiates media, RTP carries voice media, and QoS flows provide appropriate treatment for conversational traffic.

48

EPS Fallback

UE→NG-RAN→5GC→Data / Service

When VoNR is unavailable or not preferred, an IMS voice call can trigger EPS fallback from NR/5GC toward LTE/EPC, where IMS voice is established using the LTE path.

49

RAT Fallback

UE→NG-RAN→5GC→Data / Service

RAT fallback moves the UE from NR to E-UTRA while retaining 5GC connectivity. It can use RRC release with redirection or handover depending on the deployment and procedure.

50

NSA Mobility and EN-DC

NSA mobility includes LTE-master/NR-secondary management, secondary-node addition/modification/release, measurement reporting and bearer coordination. MCG, SCG and split bearers must be understood when analysing EN-DC traces.

51

Network Planning

Engineering planning considers operating band, NR-ARFCN, GSCN, slot format, antenna configuration, transmit power, PCI, cyclic prefix, CSI-RS, PT-RS, PRACH, neighbours, tracking areas, RAN notification areas and throughput expectations.

52

PCI Planning

Physical Cell Identity planning must avoid PCI collision and PCI confusion. Neighbour relationships and reuse patterns should be checked so neighbouring cells can be distinguished correctly.

53

PRACH Planning

PRACH planning includes format, configuration index, root sequence, zero-correlation zone, frequency allocation, cell radius, mobility assumptions and root-sequence reuse. Poor planning can create random-access collisions.

54

Dynamic Spectrum Sharing

UE→NG-RAN→5GC→Data / Service

DSS allows LTE and NR to coexist on a shared carrier. Scheduler coordination, LTE CRS overhead, NR resource restrictions, frequency alignment and performance impacts must be considered.

55

Measurements and KPIs

Important radio measurements include SS-RSRP, SS-RSRQ and SINR. Engineering analysis should correlate radio quality with throughput, BLER, retransmissions, latency, handover performance and accessibility.

56

Testing and Troubleshooting

UE→NG-RAN→5GC→Data / Service

For each procedure, inspect logs for expected messages, timestamps, identities, cause values, radio measurements, timers and retransmissions. A useful workflow is symptom → signalling checkpoint → radio checkpoint → transport/core checkpoint → root cause → KPI impact.

57

Random Access Troubleshooting

Use the sequence: SSB detected → PRACH transmitted → RAR received → Msg3 transmitted → contention resolved → RRC setup. A failure at each stage points to a different class of RF, configuration or signalling problem.

58

Registration Troubleshooting

Check cell selection, RRC establishment, NAS Registration Request, AMF reachability, authentication, security-mode completion, registration accept and any reject cause. Correlate UE, RAN and core timestamps.

59

PDU Session Troubleshooting

Check DNN, subscription, slice selection, SMF response, UPF selection, N4 configuration, PDU Session Establishment Accept and GTP-U tunnel creation. A successful registration does not guarantee successful session establishment.

60

Mobility Troubleshooting

Check measurement events, serving/target cell quality, neighbour configuration, target admission, handover command, random access at target, path switch and data forwarding. Distinguish radio failure from transport/core failure.

61

Voice Troubleshooting

For VoNR, verify IMS registration, SIP signalling, SDP/media negotiation, voice capability, QoS-flow creation and RTP. For fallback, verify the trigger, target LTE availability and continuation of IMS signalling.

62

5G-Advanced / Release 18

UE→NG-RAN→5GC→Data / Service

5G-Advanced extends the 5G platform with areas including XR, AI/ML, edge computing, network slicing enhancements, NTN, positioning, RedCap, sidelink evolution, energy saving, industrial IoT, UAV-related capabilities and multicast/broadcast improvements.

63

5G-Advanced / Release 19 Evolution

UE→NG-RAN→5GC→Data / Service

Treat Release 19 evolution separately from foundational Release-15 theory. Areas include AI/ML enhancements for NG-RAN and mobility, NTN evolution, positioning, MIMO evolution, XR/media, ambient IoT, network intelligence and automation.

64

Practical Engineer's Checklist

For trace analysis, always establish the timeline, identify UE/cell/core entities, verify synchronization and access, follow RRC/NAS/NGAP/SBI/user-plane layers, check radio measurements and timers, locate the first failure—not merely the final reject—and confirm the root cause with a second independent trace or KPI where possible.
